Answer key (instructor copy — remove before distributing)

Q Type Answer
1 MC B — get_GSOD() from GSODR
2 Short Duluth International Airport, station 727450-14913; daily records back to 1948 (~78 years)
3 MC B — get_GSOD() returns 40+ columns; we only need a handful
4 Short R is case-sensitive; NOAA’s column is uppercase TEMP, not temp. Run names(dlh_temp_df) to confirm, then select(TEMP)
5 T/F FALSE — summarizing trades detail for clarity; it reveals the climate signal rather than “losing” the analysis
6 Short YEARMODA is a single combined date value (useful for plotting on a continuous time axis); YEAR/MONTH/DAY are the same date split into three separate numeric columns (useful for group_by())
7 MC B — dplyr
8 Predict ~78 rows (one per year, 1948–2025)
9 Describe A dense, fuzzy band of ~28,000 overlapping points driven by the seasonal cycle (summer highs, winter lows); no long-term trend is visible — it’s buried in seasonal noise
10 Describe The seasonal cycle becomes a clean, regular wave (~936 points instead of ~28,000); a faint upward trend line is visible but the seasonal swing still dominates the y-axis range
11 Describe With only 78 points (one per year) the seasonal noise is completely averaged out, and a clear overall warming trend (upward-sloping firebrick line) is visible
12 Predict “shoulder” — summer and winter are defined by 3 months each (6 months total), so the other 6 months (spring + fall) fall into “shoulder,” giving it roughly twice the rows of either single season
13 Predict Duluth’s annual mean temperature is increasing over time at a statistically significant rate (about 0.25 °C/decade) — the trend is very unlikely to be due to chance alone
14 Describe Compare the steepness of the two geom_smooth() lines, not their starting height — the line with the steeper slope is the season warming faster, regardless of which season starts warmer or colder
15 Error == compares element-wise against a vector and only matches the first element; use MONTH %in% c(6, 7, 8) ~ "summer"
16 Error Column names from get_GSOD() are uppercase; run names(dlh_temp_df) and use select(TEMP, YEAR, MONTH)
17 Error mean() returns NA if any value in the group is NA; add na.rm = TRUE: mean(TEMP, na.rm = TRUE)
18 Error coef(model)[1] is the intercept, not the slope; the slope (rate of change per year) is coef(model)[2]
19 Error It runs, but each row is still a single day, so the model’s residuals still carry seasonal swings (~30°C) that have nothing to do with the long-term trend — the slope estimate is noisier and the seasonal variation is not actually the signal of interest; fitting on the yearly summary removes that noise first
20 Error color = season is missing from aes(); without it, ggplot() has no way to know the points/lines should be split and colored by group
21 Error season == c("summer", "winter") compares element-wise (recycled) rather than testing membership; use season %in% c("summer", "winter")
22 Concept H0: β = 0 (no linear trend in temperature over time); Ha: β ≠ 0 (temperature is changing over time); Prediction is a student’s own personal guess about the direction (e.g. “I predict it’s gotten warmer”), distinct from the hypothesis which is the formal statistical statement being tested
23 Concept Raw daily data mixes a large seasonal swing (~30°C) and day-to-day weather noise in with the much smaller long-term trend; summarizing to yearly means averages out the noise so the small, real trend becomes visible and estimable
24 Concept Assumptions include linearity, normality of residuals, constant variance, and independence of errors; yearly time-series data risks violating independence because consecutive years are not random independent draws — a warm year is more likely to be followed by another warm year (autocorrelation), which ordinary lm() does not account for
25 Concept Multiplying the yearly slope by 10 converts it to °C per decade; a slope that looks tiny year-to-year (0.02°C) compounds over the 78-year record into roughly 1.5–2°C of total warming, which is a large and biologically meaningful shift
26 Concept E.g. shrinking snow/ice cover reduces reflectivity (albedo), so more solar energy is absorbed in winter/spring, amplifying winter warming relative to summer (ice-albedo feedback); accept any physically reasonable mechanism
27 Concept Advantage: each model gives a clean, directly interpretable slope for that season alone, easy to compute and explain; harder to test directly: whether the two slopes are statistically significantly different from each other — that requires an interaction term (lm(TEMP ~ YEAR * season)) rather than two separate models
28 Applied E.g. (1) the new station ID might have large gaps in its record — check with glimpse()/count() per year before trusting the trend; (2) the station might have too short a record to detect a trend reliably — check the number of years and look at the scatter around the fitted line before interpreting the slope. Accept any reasonable data-quality concern with a matching check
