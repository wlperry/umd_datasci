Answer key (instructor copy — remove before distributing)

Q Type Answer
1 MC B — between ÷ within (signal ÷ noise)
2 T/F TRUE — 3 tests at α = 0.05 gives ~14% family-wise error
3 Short 6 pairwise tests (choose(4,2)); stacks to roughly a 26% chance of at least one false positive vs. 5% for one ANOVA
4 MC B — species (categorical) is the predictor, body_mass_g (numeric) is the response
5 Short Between = how far apart the group means are; within = how much individuals scatter inside each group
6 T/F TRUE
7 Match 1–B, 2–D, 3–A, 4–E, 5–C
8 MC B — unbalanced n per species; Type II is order-independent
9 Predict 3 rows (Adelie, Chinstrap, Gentoo) with columns species and n
10 Predict 3 boxplots (one per species) with jittered points overlaid; Gentoo visibly higher/heavier than Adelie and Chinstrap, which overlap more with each other
11 Predict 1: Residuals vs Fitted (equal variance/patterns); 2: Normal Q-Q (normality of residuals); 3: Scale-Location (spread of residuals); 4: Residuals vs Leverage (influential points)
12 Predict Tells you: at least one species mean differs from the others. Does NOT tell you: which species differ — needs post-hoc
13 Predict 3 rows — one per pairwise comparison: Adelie–Chinstrap, Adelie–Gentoo, Chinstrap–Gentoo, each with estimate, SE, df, t.ratio, p.value
14 Predict Table shows species, emmean, SE, CI, and .group; Adelie and Chinstrap share letter “a” (not different), Gentoo has “b” (different from both)
15 Predict p = 0.34 > 0.05 → variances are not significantly different → proceed with the classic ANOVA (no need for Welch’s)
16 Predict 3 points (one per species) with vertical error bars = 95% CI of the estimated mean; heavily overlapping bars would suggest those two species’ means are not clearly distinguishable
17 Error library(car) is missing; lowercase anova() is base R (Type I, compares models), capital Anova() (car) gives the Type II/III table
18 Error The formula is backwards — it models species (categorical) as a function of body mass, which is not the research question; fix: lm(body_mass_g ~ species, data = penguins_df)
19 Error shapiro.test() is being run on the model object itself, not on its residuals; fix: shapiro.test(residuals(mass_model)) — ANOVA’s normality assumption is about the residuals, not raw data or the model
20 Error penguins still has rows with missing body_mass_g/species; lm() will silently drop incomplete rows itself, but sample sizes and downstream count()/plots may not match; the lecture drops NA explicitly for a clean, documented penguins_df
21 Error Forgot pairwise ~ species — with just ~ species, emmeans() only returns estimated means, no $contrasts element to extract
22 Error cld() needs the emmeans grid, not the whole emmGrid+contrasts list; fix: multcomp::cld(mass_emm$emmeans, Letters = letters)
23 Error leveneTest() expects numeric_response ~ grouping_factor; written backwards it tests variance of a categorical variable across body-mass “groups,” which is meaningless; fix: leveneTest(body_mass_g ~ species, data = penguins_df)
24 Error type must be the character values "I", "II", or "III" (Roman numerals), not "2"
25 Concept H₀: μ_Adelie = μ_Chinstrap = μ_Gentoo (all species means equal); Hₐ: at least one species mean differs
26 Concept A hypothesis is the formal H₀/Hₐ statistical framework being tested; a prediction is your specific expectation about the data (e.g., “Gentoo will be heaviest”) — a hypothesis can be true while your particular prediction about direction/magnitude is wrong
27 Concept Independence (study design, not testable from data) / Normality of residuals (Q-Q plot + Shapiro-Wilk on residuals(model)) / Equal variance (Residuals vs Fitted pattern + Levene’s test)
28 Concept Can conclude: at least one species mean differs from the others. Cannot conclude: which specific species differ, or that all three differ from each other
29 Concept Gentoo (b) differs from both Adelie and Chinstrap (a); Adelie and Chinstrap share a letter, so they do not differ
30 Concept With large n, Shapiro-Wilk has high power and can flag even tiny, practically meaningless departures from normality as “significant”; trust the Q-Q panel — if points track the line closely, proceed
31 Concept With one predictor, Type I/II/III all agree, so the habit costs nothing here — but with two or more predictors (two-way ANOVA), the sums-of-squares type changes the answer, especially for unbalanced designs, so it’s safer to build the type = "II" habit now
32 Applied Model: “Body mass differed significantly among the three penguin species (one-way ANOVA: F(2, 339) = 343.6, p < 0.001). Tukey-adjusted comparisons showed all three species differed except Adelie and Chinstrap, with Gentoo heaviest.”
