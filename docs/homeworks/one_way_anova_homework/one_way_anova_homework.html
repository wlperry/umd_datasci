Homework 14 — One-Way ANOVA

How to turn it in

Quarto homework — fill in this .qmd, Render, submit both files.

The hypothesis, prediction, and interpretation questions below (Tasks 7 and 8) must be answered by hand on paper, photographed clearly, and embedded as images in your rendered report (see the tip at Task 7 for how to insert a photo into a .qmd). Typed answers to those two tasks receive at most half credit, even if correct — I want to see your own handwriting and your own reasoning develop on the page, not a pasted answer. Everything else (code, plots, numeric results) is typed as usual.

TipLoading the data — a built-in package (no file!) 
library(tidyverse)
library(palmerpenguins)
library(car)       # Anova(), leveneTest()
library(emmeans)   # post-hoc
penguins_df <- penguins %>% drop_na(flipper_length_mm, species)

Task 1 — Explore (2 pts)

Make a boxplot of flipper_length_mm by species. In class we used body mass — here use flipper length. Predict, before testing: do all three species differ?

Task 2 — Fit + assumptions (2 pts)

Fit lm(flipper_length_mm ~ species). Check the residual QQ plot and run leveneTest(). In one sentence each: do normality and equal variance look OK?

Task 3 — The test (2 pts)

Print the Type II ANOVA table with car::Anova(model, type = "II"). Report F, both df, and the p-value. Do you reject H₀?

Task 4 — The twist: which differ? (2 pts)

Run emmeans(model, pairwise ~ species) and read the pairwise comparisons. Which species pairs differ, and which (if any) do not?

Task 5 — Report (2 pts)

Write one results sentence: test type, F(df1, df2), p, and which species has the longest flippers.

Push it further — your own subset

Everyone above just ran the identical analysis on the full dataset, so everyone’s F, p, and letters match. Not anymore: pick one sex — "male" or "female" — and re-run the analysis on only that half of the data. Write down which sex you picked before you start.

Task 6 — Refit on your subset (3 pts)

# Replace ___ with "male" or "female" — your choice
penguins_sub <- penguins_df %>% filter(sex == "___")

Refit lm(flipper_length_mm ~ species, data = penguins_sub), re-run car::Anova(type = "II"), and re-run the emmeans post-hoc comparison on this subset. Report the new F, df, p-value, and which species pairs differ now. State your sample size (nrow(penguins_sub)) — it should be noticeably smaller than Task 3’s.

Task 7 — Predict, then check (handwritten, 3 pts)

Before you run Task 6, on paper, by hand, write down:

  1. Which sex you chose and why (just pick one — there’s no wrong answer).
  2. Your prediction: will restricting to one sex change which species pairs differ, compared to your full-data result in Task 4? Will the F-statistic likely get bigger or smaller, and why (think about what happens to sample size and to within-group variance when you remove half the data)?

Then run Task 6. Still by hand, write:

  1. Your actual F-statistic, p-value, and which pairs differ now — copied from your own real output, not a guess.
  2. Were you right? If your prediction and your result don’t match, say so honestly and explain what you think you got wrong about how ANOVA responds to a smaller, more homogeneous subset.

Photograph your handwritten page(s) and embed them, e.g.:

![My Task 7 prediction and result](task7_handwritten.jpg)

Task 8 — Explain it, by hand (handwritten, 3 pts)

On the same handwritten page(s), answer using your own actual numbers from Task 6 (not the class example, not generic values):

  1. State H₀ and Hₐ for your sex-subset test in Task 6.
  2. Using your real post-hoc/compact-letter result from Task 6, explain in plain language which species differ and which don’t — and explain why a significant overall F-test by itself would not have told you that.
  3. Compare your Task 6 F-statistic and p-value to your Task 3 (full-data) F-statistic and p-value. In your own words, explain why they differ (or why they’re surprisingly similar) given that you cut your sample size roughly in half.
